What gives it the Istanbul atmosphere

You said "catch the atmosphere, not the same exact things." These are the elements that make a player think Istanbul without copying every building.

🏛️
Stone and plasterWarm beige and grey stone on older buildings. Cracked plaster on side walls. Iron balconies. The aqueduct itself is weathered limestone with moss in the cracks.
🕌
Minarets on the skylineTwo or three mosques visible in the distance, pencil-thin minarets breaking the roofline. They don't need to be close, just silhouetted against the sky.
🚕
Yellow taxis and trafficIstanbul's yellow taxis are iconic. A few weaving through traffic, plus city buses and a tram on the boulevard, sell the scene fast.
🌳
Plane trees on the medianAtatürk Bulvarı has rows of mature plane trees down the middle. Dappled light through the canopy is a strong visual cue.
🌅
Golden-hour lightWarm, low-angle sunlight. Long shadows. Slightly hazy. This is cheap to set up in any engine and does more for atmosphere than extra geometry.
🐦
Sound and motionSeagulls near the water, the call to prayer in the distance, honking. Pigeons on the aqueduct. Small details that cost almost nothing but make the world feel alive.

How the repeating section works

The tile needs to loop without the player noticing a seam. Three tricks make this work:

1. Road alignmentBoth ends of the tile are perfectly straight and at the same elevation, so the next copy connects flush. A slight curve in the middle hides the repetition.
2. Prop shufflingThe tile has slots: awning color, parked car type, shop sign, tree size. Each copy randomizes these slots at spawn, so two adjacent copies look different even though the geometry is the same.
3. MirroringAlternating tiles are flipped left-to-right. The player sees ABAB instead of AAAA, doubling apparent variety for free.
4. Despawn behindOld tiles are removed once the bus passes them, so memory stays constant. On mobile this matters.
